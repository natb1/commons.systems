Onboarding · greenfield proposal · 2026-09-02

The Disposition Graph

Record what you want once, at the authority you mean. Let an AI work from it for months without drifting from it, exceeding it, or losing track of what needs you.

For a practitioner recording intentions for long-horizon AI dispatch Status proposal, not yet ratified Reading time 25 minutes for the core, more with the disclosures

The problem and what solving it buys

Why this model

An AI that works autonomously for a long time needs a record of what you want that stays true while it works. Three things go wrong with the obvious way of writing that record down, and each one has cost this project real weeks.

Guidance decays

Plans, file-and-line anchors, model recommendations and "do it this way" notes are written for one moment. Six weeks later they are wrong, and a session that trusts them does the wrong work confidently.

So: only what you want persists. How to get there is regenerated at the moment of work and never stored.

Authority leaks

If the AI can change any detail it was trusted with, one detail at a time, an implementation choice can quietly become a decision you never made. Priority is the sharpest case: a reshuffle can put the AI's own work above yours.

So: the AI acts only inside what you ratified, and can never rank its own work above yours or overwrite your answer with its own.

Your queue is scattered

Decisions waiting on you end up in parks, notes, proposals and stamps across the record. Nobody, including you, can see the whole list, so the list grows stale and the AI stalls on things you would have answered in a minute.

So: one derived queue. Everything that needs you is computed from the record, never kept in a second store.

The model that follows is small on purpose. There is one kind of record, one hierarchical edge, three named references, and a handful of properties. Everything else, including priority, context, and your review queue, is derived from those. When something is derived it cannot go stale on its own, and that is the whole game.

What it replaces, in one paragraph

The incumbent graph groups many decisions under one node (a strategy with hundreds of clarifications), stores priority inputs and status values that drift, keeps three separate stores for things waiting on the author (office-hours parks, gap notes, deferred stamps), and needs a set of hand-applied decomposition rules to keep node boundaries meaningful. The transient tactic layer that carried plans is already retired by the record. This proposal finishes the move: the decision becomes the node, priority and placement are computed, the author's queue is derived, and the decomposition rules disappear because there is nothing left for them to decide.

The one kind of record

What a node is

A node is one question and its current answer. Not a topic, not a component, not a strategy with a list of decisions inside it. If a text answers two questions, it is two nodes. If a new answer replaces an old one, the node holds the new answer and git holds the old. That is the whole definition of "same scope": two things belong in one node only if they answer the same question, which means one of them replaced the other.

The answer is written as accumulated current position, the way a legal restatement is written: what holds now, why, and what was rejected. The history of how it got there lives in version control, which is already the archive for everything else.

The fields

Nothing else is stored. Rank, the context a session loads, whether a node is "done", who owns it, which tradition nodes point back at it, where it sits in the file tree, and your review queue are all computed from the fields above.

Why the answer has forms instead of the record having kinds

The incumbent record separates virtues, strategies, criteria and conditions as kinds. Under this model those are what different answers do when their instrument fails. A target that fails becomes work. A rule that fails becomes a variance that gates the work that broke it. An assumption that fails sends the question above it back to you, because the answer no longer makes sense in the world as observed. A care never fails and never completes; it is why everything below it exists. The form tells the reconciler what a failure means, and that is all the old kinds were doing.

Whether a node is a "virtue" or a "strategy" is then derived: a root whose answer is a care reads as a virtue; a refinement whose answer carries assumptions and targets reads as a strategy. See question five below for the argument.

Procedure

Recording an intention

This is a sequence, and the order matters: each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Find the question

    Write the question someone would have to ask you to get this answer. If you cannot phrase it as one question, you have two dispositions. A tell: if part of your text is something you decided and another part is something you would let the AI decide, it is two questions.

    Bad: "Publishing platform". Good: "Does the site build reproducibly from the repository alone?"

  2. Place it under the question it refines

    Ask "why does this question exist?" The answer names its parent. If it exists for two reasons, name both. Never choose a parent to change priority; that is what boost is for, and it is yours alone.

  3. Answer it in the form it takes

    A target, a rule, an assumption, or a care. Write the current position, then the rationale: why, and what you rejected. Rejected alternatives are part of the answer, not a footnote.

  4. Stamp the authority you mean

    Ratified if you decided and want to be asked before it changes. Delegated if you are handing the class of decision to the AI and do not want to be asked again. The AI stamps its own answers deferred, and records anything that would need your authority as a proposal.

  5. Ground and cite

    Name the traditions the answer rests on and how (adopted, diverged, chosen over). Name the other dispositions you relied on; the record pins their content so you learn when they move.

  6. Bind an instrument

    Say how you would know it holds: a check the machine can run, or an assessment that expires and gets redone. Skip this and the answer is unguarded: the AI can act under it, but nothing will tell you when it stops being true.

Three records, written out

The syntax is illustrative. The fields are the point.

# a root: what you hold permanently
id:        own-tools
question:  What do I hold onto regardless of circumstance?
answer:    care — I keep the ability to run my own publishing without a vendor's permission.
rationale: A platform I cannot leave is a platform that owns my audience. Rejected: "use whatever is easiest"; ease is a price, not a value.
authority: ratified, author, 2026-09-02
grounds:   [{tradition: free-software, relation: adopted, note: "the four freedoms, applied to hosting"}]

# a refinement you decided, with an assumption and a check
id:        site-builds-from-repo
question:  Does the site build reproducibly from the repository alone?
under:     [own-tools]
answer:    target — a clean checkout produces the deployed site with one command.
rationale: Reproducibility is the property that makes leaving a host a DNS change. Rejected: hosted build service (adds a dependency exactly where the care forbids one).
authority: ratified, author, 2026-09-02
instrument: check ci/build-from-clean
cites:     [{id: own-tools, hash: 9f1c…}]

# a detail you handed to the AI, and its answer
id:        static-generator
question:  Which static site generator builds it?
under:     [site-builds-from-repo]
answer:    target — Eleventy, pinned in package.json.
rationale: Plain templates, no framework runtime, active maintenance. Rejected: Hugo (Go toolchain adds a second build language to maintain).
authority: delegated, claude, 2026-09-02
instrument: check ci/build-from-clean   # shares its parent's check

Notice what is not there: no plan, no steps, no file paths, no "recommended model", no status field, no priority number. The AI regenerates the plan when it claims the work, and the record cannot go stale on any of those.

Safety

What the AI may change

Delegation only works if the AI can act repeatedly without asking, and it is only safe if the AI cannot turn what you delegated into something you did not. Every tradition that has faced this settled on the same shape: a delegate acts freely within a recorded scope, can only pass on less than it received, and can never amend the grant that created its authority. Here the scope is your ratified answers, read flat across the whole record. Nothing new is encoded for it.

You

Ratified

Changes only through an interview with you. Caps the rank of everything non-ratified beneath it.

The AI, in a class you handed over

Delegated

The AI answers and re-answers at will, as long as it stays consistent with every ratified answer. No review item is minted for that.

The AI, in a class you did not

Deferred

Same freedom and the same cap, but a review is owed. The AI acts on it while you catch up.

Recorded, not in force

Proposal

What the AI writes when it thinks a ratified answer should change, or when its answer would contradict one. Inert until you rule.

Exercise

Replacing a delegated or deferred answer with another one, or adding delegated child questions, consistent with every standing ratified answer: allowed, and it changes nothing about the review queue. If you did not care about the detail, you still do not.

Out of scope

An answer inconsistent with any ratified answer cannot be recorded with authority at all. It is recorded as a proposal, which is inert. This is the fix for the breakout you named: a deferred disposition is still a disposition and can be acted on, so a contradicting answer must not be one.

No write up

A non-ratified answer never becomes the standing answer on a ratified question. A non-ratified subtree's rank is capped at its nearest ratified ancestor's rank. Delegated re-parenting stays inside the same ratified subtree. Lowering a delegated rank is always allowed; raising it is bounded.

Guards exist

Oversight questions (pause, pace, the review queue, stopping a session, permission modes) are ratified at the global tier with instruments, so they are in every delegated act's scope by construction. A ratified answer without an instrument is flagged as unguarded.

Where each rule comes from

Exercise within scope is administrative law's enabling act and Ostrom's nested rule levels: operational rules change within collective-choice rules, which change within constitutional rules. Out of scope is void is the doctrine of ultra vires; a "Henry VIII clause", delegated power that can amend the enabling act, is the named anti-pattern. No write up is the Biba integrity model and object-capability attenuation: a delegate can only pass on less. The rank ceiling and containment are cgroup v2: a delegated subtree manager can only move things inside its own subtree, and a child's share is a fraction of its parent's; the nice/CAP_SYS_NICE asymmetry supplies "lowering is free, raising is bounded". Guards at the global tier is the conclusion of the corrigibility literature (Soares et al. 2015; the off-switch game, Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017): oversight settings live outside the agent's alterable scope. The deferred queue is Christiano's approval-directed agency, and the sampled audit of exercises is the AI-control tradition (Greenblatt et al. 2023).

Operation

How work happens

The AI never reads a backlog you maintain. It derives one. Each cycle does the same seven things, and each is a function of the record plus what has been observed.

1 · deriveFrontier

Every disposition in a "needs work" state: a target whose check fails, a question with no answer the AI is allowed to give, a citation whose content moved, a shim past its end condition, a variance from a broken rule.

2 · orderRank

Attention share flows down under; your boost adjusts it; the nearest ratified ancestor caps non-ratified items.

3 · claimOne item

The top item is claimed exclusively for one session. Its strategy is derived, not selected.

4 · loadContext

Its ancestors, the global tier, and what it cites. Nothing else, and never a stored summary that could be stale.

5 · plan and doExecute

The plan is generated now and dies with the claim. Any decision made on the way is recorded as delegated, deferred, or a proposal.

6 · observeEvidence

What happened, with proof, appended to the operational log. Never edited.

7 · compactFold

The evidence log is folded into current observed state. Git keeps the rest.

Two things in this loop are deliberately absent. There is no standing list of tasks: the frontier is recomputed, so finishing or abandoning work never leaves a stale item behind. And there is no stored context: what a session sees is derived from its position in the graph, so the same question always loads the same ancestry.

Your side of the loop

What comes back to you

One list, derived every time it is shown, ordered by the same rank the AI uses:

  • Proposals. The AI thinks one of your answers should change, or could not answer without contradicting one. Nothing has happened yet.
  • Deferred answers. The AI decided something in a class you had not delegated. It is acting on it. You can ratify, re-answer, or delegate the class so you are not asked again.
  • Unanswered questions that need you. Reading sittings, decisions with no delegated scope, anything owner-only.
  • Assumptions that failed. The world changed under one of your answers, so the question above it is back to you.
  • Stale intent. Something a ratified answer cited has moved.
  • Unguarded answers. Ratified, with no instrument.

The list is the whole contract in the other direction. If it is empty, the AI is working entirely inside what you ratified and delegated. If it is long, that is information about your record, not about the AI.

This revision

Six questions, six answers

Your review of the first draft asked six things. Each changed the model, and the change is now in the sections above. The reasoning is here.

A node holds the answer's history? Other evaluations concluded history belongs to git, citing common-law precedent accumulation. Is this thrashing?

It was thrashing, and the recorded conclusion wins. History is dropped from the node.

I imported an incumbent habit, "clarifications append, never rewrite", which made sense when a node was a cluster of many decisions. With one question per node, a re-answer replaces the standing answer and the previous one is in git, which is the reporter in the common-law analogy. The node carries accumulated state: the current answer, its rationale, what it rejected, and at most one pending proposal. No function needs the old answer in the node. The one apparent exception, the overrule algebra's "superseded stamp survives", is satisfied by git, and the case where it mattered most (a non-ratified answer replacing a ratified one) is now forbidden outright rather than recorded.

"Same scope means answers the same question, so a node's cluster is over time, never over subjects." Help me understand this.

It means a node is one disposition, and the "cluster" was a leftover.

Earlier drafts treated a node as a set of dispositions sharing properties, so "same scope" had to say which properties. With scope defined as the question, the only way two dispositions share a node is if they answer the same question, and two standing answers to one question cannot both hold, so one replaced the other. That is what "over time" meant. Now that history lives in git, even that collapses: a node is exactly one disposition, plus an optional proposal. The grouping you wanted, all the questions about one subject side by side, is what the subtree under a question gives you, and it is a read view, not a record boundary.

"Ultra vires records as deferred and queues" sounds like an authority breakout. A deferred disposition is still a disposition; the AI can act on it pending review.

Correct, and the draft was wrong. Out-of-scope answers record as proposals, with no authority.

Deferred now means exactly one thing: an answer the AI gave inside your ratified scope, in a class you had not delegated, acting while review is owed. Anything that would need ratified authority, because it contradicts a ratified answer, would replace one, or would move a subtree across a ratified boundary, is a proposal: recorded for the queue, inert until you rule. Breakout is then impossible by construction, since nothing inert acts and nothing attenuated exceeds its ceiling.

Is criterion redundant with evidence?

Not with evidence. It is redundant with disposition.

Evidence is what was observed, appended, with no authority. A criterion is what should be true, authored, with authority. The frontier is the difference between them, so neither can replace the other. But a criterion was never a separate kind of thing from a disposition: it is a disposition whose answer is a target and which has an instrument bound to it. The record's vocabulary kept CRITERION and DISPOSITION apart because a strategy node held many decisions and only some were "acceptance conditions". With one disposition per node, every disposition can carry an instrument, a check or an assessment, and the word criterion adds nothing. The functional, non-functional, and assumption classes survive as the form of the answer and as the global tier.

Does the virtue/strategy distinction survive a functional evaluation?

As a derived property, yes. As an authored kind, no.

What the distinction does: virtues are roots, never complete, carry no assumptions, ground the subtree, and rank at the top tier. Strategies refine a virtue, rest on assumptions, and have satisfiable targets beneath them. Every one of those is readable off the record: root versus refinement from under, permanence from the answer's form (care), tier from depth. The incumbent's "the serves edge is the one phase change, disposition becomes state" is the observation that strategies have targets; it does not need a kind field to be true. Retiring the field removes a placement judgment and a validator rule. The mounts (delegations, traditions) are a different case: the decision to delegate is a disposition under a virtue, but the delegatee itself is a referent, an id in a stamp or a grounds entry, and needs no node of its own unless something is worth recording about it. Confidence is moderate; see the open questions for the rank-tier consequence.

If this is a DAG and a node can be under many nodes, why is cites required for cousin reference?

Because under asserts motivation and cites does not, and motivation moves rank.

Putting a question under a second parent says "this exists because of that" and has three consequences: it draws attention share from the second parent, loads the second parent's whole ancestry as context, and takes the second parent's ratified ceiling. A delegated detail that merely relied on a ruling elsewhere would, under a second parent, inherit rank from a strategy that does not motivate it. That is the placement-for-rank workaround the record already refused. A citation carries the dependency without the motivation: it loads the cited disposition into context and pins its content so you learn when it moves, and it changes nothing about rank. One refinement fell out of your question: a parent's answer can also move, so under gets the same content pin at decision time. The two references differ in one bit, motivation, and I have kept two names rather than a flag because a reader should not have to inspect a flag to know whether rank is involved.

Where the confidence runs out

Open questions

Each item below is a proposal made with less than full confidence, with the context that supports it, so you can rule on it directly. The confidence label is honest: "record" means the record already says it; "tradition" means a named tradition backs it and the record does not contradict it; "drafting" means it is mine.

Kind as a derived property drafting

Retiring the authored kind removes a placement judgment, but the incumbent rank key uses tier explicitly (boosts are keyed by tier, "tier 1" means top strategies). If tier becomes depth under under, a deep-but-important question ranks low unless boosted. Alternative: keep a ratified tier property on a few nodes. Recommendation: derive tier from depth and let boost carry the exceptions, since that is what boost is for. Cost if wrong: a handful of boosts to author.

Ranking namespaces as an allocation, not a strategy drafting, overrules a refusal

On 2026-08-11 you refused to exempt a ranking-namespace child strategy from carrying its own success signal, to keep ungrounded nodes out of the strategy layer. Under this model a namespace is a ratified allocation (a boost on a question) whose instrument is "the allocation holds", so it is grounded without being a strategy. Recommendation: accept; the refusal's concern is met differently. If you keep the refusal, namespaces must be questions with targets, which works but re-introduces the node the refusal wanted to avoid.

Keeping after as a precedence hint tradition, with a real divergence

Level-triggered reconciliation (Kubernetes) has no explicit dependencies: a target whose precondition is unmet simply fails its check until the precondition holds, and the tradition accepts the wasted loops. Here a wasted loop is a claimed session and its tokens, so an optional hint is cheaper. It never affects rank except through the existing rule that a blocker of a ranked item inherits that item's rank. Recommendation: keep, optional, delegated. Cost if wrong: hints go stale, and a stale hint is a blocked frontier item you would see in the queue.

Folding recovers into the delegation's own targets drafting

Today a strategy names the delegation records its work unwinds. Under this model the delegation decision is a disposition under a virtue, and "is the path back from this delegatee open?" is a target on it; recovery work appends evidence there. A recovery strategy sits under the virtue, not under the delegatee, because the delegatee is the object of the work and not its motivation, and an under edge to it would count recovery work as captured. Recommendation: fold. Worth one question, because capture accounting is the thing this edge existed to inform.

Consistency is judged, not computed record, stated honestly

"Consistent with every ratified answer" cannot be checked mechanically. It is judged at record time by the adversarial-review duty, with the ratified ancestry and global tier in context, and it is observed afterward by instruments: a delegated change that breaks a ratified target shows up as a failing check. That is the status quo. The proposal adds only that oversight answers are global-tier with instruments, so the shutdown case is observed. Recommendation: accept the limit and add a sampled audit of exercises, not only of overrides.

Legacy unstamped dispositions during the drain author-stated shim, one consequence to confirm

You ruled that unstamped dispositions carry authority class null and are formalized on adjacent alignment. If null meant "no authority", the entire legacy record would go inert overnight. So the shim must let null-class dispositions act as delegated until formalized, with the attenuation rules applying to them. A legacy disposition that contradicts a ratified answer is then already a stale-projection frontier item under authority primacy. Recommendation: state that consequence in the shim explicitly.

Never fold authority, only projections drafting

Design rationale is the largest persisting content class and grows without bound. Consolidation should compact projections (the subtree summary a reader sees) and never merge dispositions into one another, because a merge would silently average authority stamps and rationale. Recommendation: ratify the "never fold authority" half now; delegate the compaction mechanics.

Two pinned references or one drafting

under and cites both pin content now and differ in one bit, motivation. A single reference type with a motivates flag is more parsimonious on paper. I kept two names so that rank involvement is visible without reading a flag. Either is fine; the choice is presentational.

Not onboarding

Appendix for the ratifying interview

Everything below is for the interview that ratifies this model, not for a new practitioner. It is collapsed so it does not compete with the guide.

Principles for ratification, numbered
  1. Disposition is the unit. A node is one question and its standing answer, plus at most one proposal. Same scope = same question. History is git.
  2. One hierarchical edge. under carries refinement, attention containment, context, and the authority ceiling because those are readings of one fact. serves and parent merge. Multi-parent allowed. Pinned at decision time.
  3. Store each fact once; derive the rest. No stored rank, floor, status, owner, kind, mirror, or placement.
  4. Attenuation. Exercise within scope; out of scope records as an inert proposal; no write up (standing answer, rank ceiling, containment). Consistency is judged globally. Ratified ancestry plus the global tier is the scope; nothing new is encoded for it.
  5. Guards exist by construction. Oversight questions are ratified at the global tier with instruments. Unguarded ratified answers surface on the frontier.
  6. Overload topology only for readings of one fact. Attention that diverges from refinement uses boost; global applicability is tier; cousin dependency is cites.
  7. Rationale and rejected options are part of the answer. Plans, anchors, model picks, and measurements are projections or evidence, never dispositions.
  8. Every disposition can carry an instrument (check or assessment); the word "criterion" retires; the class axis survives as the answer's form plus the global tier.
  9. Null-authority shim during the drain (author-stated): unstamped dispositions act as delegated until formalized on adjacent alignment; unstamped under edges default to delegated. Liquidation: none remain.
  10. Rank is a disposition and rank inputs are disposition properties (author-stated); re-encoding that moves rank follows the overrule algebra for the affected inputs' class.
