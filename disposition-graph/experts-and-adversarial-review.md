**The fill test.** Adversarial review performs eleven distinguishable functions in the record: the wind-egg test, the review of one's own output as part of producing it; the purgation of the drafter's conceit, the function no party discharges on itself; the verdict that gates the author's ruling, with the readiness gate; the steelman, the case against written by a party that did not draft; the inspection of the artifact as written, for defects of composition; the frontier's self-consistency; the per-draft validations, the first six and the fifteenth, over one node and its neighbourhood; the pin, by which a reading records the text it read so that staleness is computable; the kickback, a durable node-moving verdict with residue; the gate that fires without the author spending attention; and the diff review of a materialized landing. ### The tradition survey, 2026-09-09

The second evaluation, with reference to tradition, ran as a unit on the larger model at high effort, and what it returned is written here as data for the same reason the record survey is: its report stood outside the record and dies with the sitting. It read for what the adversarial paradigm secures, what the expert paradigm secures, and what the traditions that tried to combine them learned, and it closed on the node that grounds the review, `plato-elenchus`. Its search budget was gone before it began, which `unreached-traditions` records; the readings below say for each whether the text was opened, reached second hand, or not reached at all, and a reading carried from knowledge and not from a text says so.

**What the adversarial paradigm secures.**

1. A function no party can discharge on itself, structurally and not morally. Murrie and colleagues, 2013, verified and unopened, is the empirical premise: forensic experts told which side retained them scored identical case files in that side's favour, in both directions, on structured instruments built to remove exactly that discretion, and without noticing. The Civil Procedure Rules answered with r 35.3, an overriding duty to the court on the party's own expert, and a decade of evidence in the Irish Law Reform Commission's consultation paper of 2008 is that the duty made allegiance disreputable without making it rare. A duty is not an instrument. What detected the bias in the leading case, Cala Homes, was cross-examination surfacing an article the expert had written years earlier, and Laddie J's sentence is the plainest statement of what removing the channel costs: where there is no cross-examination the court is at much greater risk of being misled. The adversary is not smarter; the adversary is differently interested, and competence does not substitute for that.
2. Discipline by anticipation, the loss no instrument this record can build will show. JF v DPP, the Irish Supreme Court in 2005: the mere presence, actual or anticipated, of an expert on the other side provides a wholesome discipline. Most of what an adversarial channel does it does upstream, in the drafting, to a party that knows it will be attacked. Two consequences cut against the change. A reviewer with a low find-rate is not evidence the office is idle, since that is equally consistent with the office working perfectly. And the cost of removing it is systematically invisible: the drafting session's private incentive changes, drafts get marginally less defensible, and nothing in the record moves, because the effect was never in the findings. The survey names this the one warning to take if only one is taken.
3. A procedural invalidation rule that competence cannot satisfy. Sanhedrin 17a, read verbatim: a capital conviction on which every judge agreed is void, because there will not be anyone arguing for the accused. The tradition does not say the unanimous court erred; it says a proceeding in which nobody argued the other side did not happen, a claim about form immune to every claim about the participants, including the demand on the same page that each judge be able to argue the rejected side. Under this rule an expert round that ends in agreement is not a passed round but an incomplete one.
4. Measured superiority on the dimension the record exists for. Schwenk's meta-analysis of 1990 and Schweiger, Sandberg and Rechner in 1989, both verified and unopened, find devil's advocacy and dialectical inquiry beat consensus and expert approaches on decision quality and on surfacing assumptions, and lose on satisfaction and acceptance: the adversarial forms win on the thing the record cares about and lose on the thing it does not. No effect size is quoted, since the texts were not opened.
5. Mill, from knowledge and not from the text. Only the first of the four limbs of the second chapter of On Liberty concerns the suppressed opinion being true; the other three bite where the received opinion is true and everyone competent, since the opinion becomes prejudice, its meaning decays into formula, and where no real opponent exists one must be manufactured, Mill's own example being the devil's advocate. Then the concession Mill makes against his own device: the manufactured opponent is second best, and he requires the contrary case from people who hold it and defend it in earnest. The expert participant is Mill's manufactured opponent, and Mill rejected the substitution in advance; on his terms the answer to `words/2026-09-08/26` is that something is lost.
6. Where the classical case is weaker than it looks. Popper constrains the claim and not the personnel, so a participant who makes a recommendation sharper and more exposed satisfies falsificationism as fully as an adversary does, and Popper favours the adversary only at second order, on severity of test; and Cole, Cole and Simon in 1981, unopened, suggest that which reviewer is drawn may dominate what posture the reviewer takes.

**What the expert paradigm secures.**

1. It answers the failure the adversarial channel actually has, which is undecidability and not lying. Woolf's final report of 1996, at 13.8 and read second hand through the Irish paper: the contest leaves the decider to choose between two contrary views, unsure that either is right, especially where the issues are technical or outside the decider's expertise, and the decision may be influenced by the apparently greater authority of one side's expert or the experts' relative fluency; the concurrent-evidence critique adds that the likelihood increases as the complexity of the question increases. This transfers exactly and is the strongest case for the author's refinement that the record had not stated: the author is Woolf's judge. The design puts the ruling with the person who by construction holds less context on any draft than the session that produced it, and an adversarial procedure ending in two well-argued incompatible positions does not inform that person but transfers the decision to whichever agent writes better, worst on the hardest nodes.
2. It removes two failure modes already visible in adversarial review output, from the Commercial judges' fault list under Woolf: polarisation of issues and unwillingness to concede from the start, and expansion from the confines of expert evidence into rival submissions. Both are structural consequences of an office measured by its findings: the reviewer that escalates to fill its remit, and the reviewer that drifts from testing the draft into redesigning it.
3. It makes concession affordable. Stanton v Callaghan, 1998, had to grant experts immunity for what they conceded in a joint statement, because otherwise the retaining party holds it against them and they will not concede; an adversarial reviewer is in that position permanently and cannot be immunised out of it, since if its usefulness is read off its findings, conceding is self-liquidating.
4. It is the older classical position. Aristotle's Rhetoric, book one, chapter one, verbatim with the Bekker lines unverified: we must be able to argue on opposite sides of a question, not in order to employ it both ways in practice, but so that we may see clearly what the facts are and confute one who argues unfairly. The two-sided capacity is a qualification of the competent participant and explicitly not a role, and Sanhedrin 17a states it as an eligibility rule for the seat. The author has not invented the expert participant but recovered the older of the two forms.
5. Cost, a consequence and not a reason under `evaluation`: Woolf named expert evidence one of the two largest generators of unnecessary cost and delay in civil litigation.
6. Where the expert case is weakest. Howard's line, locus unverified: it is slightly mysterious that experts should be thought venal when engaged by the parties and paragons of objectivity when employed by the court. Mahoney in 1977, unopened, shows the nominally neutral reviewer is not neutral; Neal in 2016, unread, raises that the adversarial structure may have been sorting a pre-existing bias rather than creating it. The expert paradigm's premise, that allegiance goes away when the office does, is the premise least supported by anything the survey read.

**On combining them.** The Civil Procedure Rules' r 35.12 is a channel and not a merger: the parties keep their own experts and are compelled to confer, disagreement is a required output with reasons under (3)(b), so a statement recording only agreement does not comply, (4) buys candour with confidentiality, and (5) denies the experts' agreement any binding force on the parties. That tradition's answer to participants agreeing too readily is not to keep them apart but to deny their agreement force, and the record is already isomorphic to it in the division between recommendation and confirmation; so the strongest thing r 35.12 says here is that the expert paradigm is safe in proportion as its output is non-authoritative and dangerous in proportion as an agreed round is read as a passed one. The merger form did not hold: within three years Daniels v Walker let a dissatisfied party obtain its own expert for reasons that are not fanciful, and Auld LJ's review of 2001 adds the boundary that no single expert is imposed where the point is genuinely in issue. What lasted was never one expert instead of two but two experts made to confer, whose agreement binds nobody, either still challengeable. Adversarial collaboration, read in full in Cowan and colleagues 2020, a collaboration that did not end in agreement, adds the precondition that the parties share some assumptions, which is the author's own common ground at `words/2026-09-08/29` minted independently, the payoff that the joint form's real product is a shared negative and not agreement, the price in the authors' own sentence, that the curtailing of bold arguments may be considered a drawback, which `boldness` being a field on every fact makes a cost the record can see if it looks, and the need for a third party committed to no position, chosen for non-commitment and not competence, which is what the reading in clean context already is. Six failure modes of the combination recur: specious consensus, Sackman and Woudenberg through Morgan 2014, read in full, whose detector is the spread, since individually elicited experts gave significantly wider ranges than the analogous consensus process, so fewer options, thinner cases against and lower boldness in an expert round is the Delphi signature and not maturity; curtailed boldness, which is true agreement bought by trimming claims; loss of the schools of thought, to which the record already holds the older answer at Eduyot 1:5, that the single opinion is recorded so that a later court may depend on it, which under the expert paradigm makes the `against` and the passed option's reason the sole custodian of the losing argument; selection for safe views, since the participant is convened by, briefed by and reports through the session holding the draft; the candid channel leaking, Aird v Prime Meridian 2006; and costing more and being more adversarial than either pure form, the concurrent-evidence hearing having been abandoned in one tribunal as too cumbersome, too expensive and too adversarial.

Six things recur across unrelated traditions and together describe what neither pure form gives: two-sided competence as the qualification for the seat; disagreement as a required output with reasons; no binding force on what the participants agree; a protected channel for the tentative thing; an uncommitted third role; and a rule treating silence in the adversarial channel as invalidating rather than confirming. The sixth is the one the record does not have and the one the change makes necessary. Every tradition that internalised the adversary kept a rule that noticed when it went silent, and none dropped the requirement that somebody argue; the survey's recommendation is neither to keep the adversarial reviewer nor to refuse the expert participant but that an expert round returning no contested ground be a signal and not a pass, since that is the one outcome an expert procedure cannot distinguish from having worked.

**On `plato-elenchus`.** The three functions come apart in the texts. The Theaetetus pin at 150b to 150c is narrower than the wind-egg doctrine it supports. The node cites six passages and omits Plato's own limit on the instrument, Republic 539b to 539d, the young using dialectic as contradiction-sport, like puppies tugging, a caution against the failure the author diagnosed; the locus is from knowledge and the wording paraphrase, since the text could not be opened. And the node's own case against concedes that Plato's answerer can refuse a premise and a file cannot, which points away from the option the node recommends: the elenchus is constitutively two-way, and a reviewer firing findings at a static draft is not conducting one. Read carefully the tradition supports the expert-participant reading better than the adversarial-reviewer reading it was minted to ground. The survey records that as tension and not as a move, and this node's account carries it to that node.
