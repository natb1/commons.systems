## Answer

Supports, whole. The tradition holds that the cheapest lever in an inspection is refusing to convene one over material that fails entry: a document is checked against stated entry criteria, mechanical and cheap, before any inspector's time is spent on it, and a document that fails is returned to its author and never inspected in that state. The answer takes that whole: the mechanical tier is the entry criteria, the survey's reader is the inspection, and a node that fails the tier is repaired or kicked back and never read for it. The tradition's own reason is the answer's: an inspector who spends attention on defects a checklist could have caught is spending the scarcest input on the cheapest finding, and the inspection's yield on the defects only a reader can find falls with every mechanical one it is asked to report. The one refinement is the tier's bound, which the tradition states as the criteria being objective and the answer states as a check entering the tier only where it is obviously right when it fires.
