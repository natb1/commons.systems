## Answer

Supports, with two divergences. The tradition holds that when a set of items shares one structure and a reader works them one at a time, the set is shown as a persistent list beside a panel that renders the selection: the list carries whatever the reader needs in order to choose what to work next, the selection is marked in the list, and the panel is where the work happens. The alignment page adopts every part of that. The rail is the list, in the one ruling order, carrying each node's question, its graph, its stage, and its settling count, which is what a reader needs to choose; the selection is marked there; and the right-hand columns are the panel.

The first divergence is that the tradition's detail is one panel and this page's is two, the decisions this ruling asks and the node as it would stand. The tradition's reason for one panel is that the reader's attention should have one object, and the divergence answers it rather than ignoring it: the two panels have one object between them, the node, and the author ruled on 2026-09-03 that the result be in view while the decisions are worked rather than reached after them.

The second divergence is on where the work is committed. Master-detail conventionally holds an item's edits in the detail panel and commits or discards them when the selection leaves it. This page stages responses across nodes and submits them together, because the unanswered node opens the three responses on any subset of the frontier at once, so leaving a node must never discard what is staged on it and the rail must mark every node that carries one.
