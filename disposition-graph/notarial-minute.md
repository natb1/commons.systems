## Answer

Supports the ruling being written where the act happened, in the words the party used, and dated; and supplies the reason it is not a copy that carries the authority. What makes a notarial act authentic is not the signature on the paper the party takes home. It is that an officer recorded the declaration as it was made, in the declarant's presence, dated it, and kept the original. Copies circulate and are useful, and every one of them is only evidence of the minute. The device separates the act from its circulation, so a disagreement between a copy and the record is settled by the record without argument.

The record takes the whole shape. A ruling is written on the option the author chose, with the response, the date, the pin of the recommendation it answered, and, where the author gave one, their reason in their own words; the words that opened or moved the dialogue stay under `## Disposition`, verbatim and dated, and nothing that arrives later transcribes them into authority. Every projection is a copy of that. The rule the record already states in the same spirit is that a ruling whose words are not in the record is invalid and that transcribing the author's words from any other record confers nothing, which is the minute's rule stated for a graph of files.

The reading-back is the half the record revives, and it was shelved by a constraint that has gone. The notary read the instrument aloud to the party before signature so that the party's consent was to what the document said and not to what they assumed it said; the requirement thinned wherever a second reader's time was the expensive thing. It is affordable when the reader is an agent, and it is the clause that has the session say, in the turn it does either, which rulings it recorded and which movement it chose, so the author's next act is on a stated account of what the last one was taken to mean.

Where the record cannot meet the tradition's condition, stated with the counter attached. The notary is an officer with an independent duty, personal liability, and no interest in the transaction, and the party is physically present. Here the party's words reach the record through the same AI that drafted what they are ruling on, so the officer and the drafter are one, and presence is a turn in a conversation. What the record has in place of the office is that only the author rules, that a denial is never a ruling, and that the session's classification of an ambiguous response is disclosed in the turn it is made. Those are real, and they are checks on the drafter by the drafter; the tradition's guarantee comes from the officer being someone else, and the record does not have that and should not claim it.
