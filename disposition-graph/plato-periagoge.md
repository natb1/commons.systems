## Answer

Yes, for the conduct in which the record is authoritative and the author is turned back to it. Socrates denies that education puts knowledge into a soul that lacks it and says instead that the capacity is already there and must be turned toward what is real (518b to d); the interview under this conduct does not implant an account but turns the author to the record and the readings, and the author articulates first. The word is the one the incumbent skill used for the same conduct.

## Rationale

Adopted as the name and the ground of the periagogic conduct. The divergence to keep in view: Plato's turning is toward the forms, while here it is toward a record the author wrote or accepted, so the authority that the turning appeals to is the author's own past decision, not a truth above the author.
