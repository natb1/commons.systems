## Account

An un-aligned disposition, recorded from the author's words the turn they were
said, in the sitting on `commons.systems/disposition-graph/alignment-page`. It
is a question of its own and not that node's, because the rule binds every
node's prose and not the page that renders it, and because its example,
rejected options, is one instance the author marked as such.

What the sitting would amend, read before anything is changed. Three standing
answers state the pattern the rule forbids. `node`, this question's parent,
says "The rationale says why, and which alternatives were rejected".
`rationale-edge` says the rationale is "why this answer stands, citing its
readings and evidence and weighing the alternatives it rejected".
`rejected`, whose recommended answer is that a rejected alternative is a
viable option kept on the fact, then licenses the prose in the same breath:
"The rationale keeps why the standing answer stands and may cite the options
it argues against, since a rationale that argues by elimination has no
argument left without them." The rule the author states is stricter than that
clause and broader than the example, and the three have to be reconciled with
it or against it.

**The measurement, which qualifies the author's diagnosis and is the
periagogic probe this node owes them.** Thirty-two of the seventy-five nodes
carry a `Rejected:` passage in a `## Rationale`, four as a bulleted list and
twenty-eight as a prose sentence naming each alternative with a clause of
reason. Across those thirty-two the passages hold about a hundred and forty
rejected candidates against a hundred and forty-five recorded answer-fact
options, and the two sets barely intersect. `viable-options` names nine
rejected candidates in prose and records three options; `alignment-order`
twelve against two; `clean-context-review` twelve against three.
`commons.systems/public/agency` names two, neither of them an option, in a
block that is byte-identical across all eight commits that touched the file
and was read by neither of the two encoding migrations.

So the prose is not mostly residue of options that failed to migrate. It
carries roughly three times what the structure carries, and most of what it
carries was never a recorded option at all. The author's diagnosis is right in
kind and understated in degree, and the difference decides the answer: a rule
that liquidates the prose without giving its contents a structural home
discards about a hundred recorded rejections, which `viable-options` forbids
recovering as options, since it holds an option viable only while nothing on
the list dominates it and these were all rejected as dominated.

The same paragraph on `viable-options` carries a second structure in prose:
eight traditions with what each is adopted for, which is what a reading node
and its `bears` entries exist to hold, and which `readings` already forbids.
So the question is not confined to rejected options even within a single node.

Three candidate answers are on the table and none is drafted. That every
candidate the AI has considered becomes an option carrying its status, so
viability is a shown property rather than the condition of being listed and
the prose keeps only argument. That the rule binds only duplication, so prose
restating what `options` already holds is liquidated and prose holding what no
structure holds stays as the rationale's argument by elimination, which is
`rejected`'s standing licence narrowed. Or that the rejections leave the record
and version control holds them, which is what `rejected` already says of an
option that stops being viable, and which the measurement prices at about a
hundred entries.

The periagogic object is those three nodes, the thirty-two rationales, and the
projector's `/^rejected\b/i` heading match in the browser template, which reads
the prose the rule would liquidate.
