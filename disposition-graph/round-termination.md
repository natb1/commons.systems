#### shared-grounding-with-typed-persuasion

A round ends when the parties hold shared grounding in its inputs — a common
understanding of tradition and of the recorded state of the graph — and not when
they agree in the conclusion. Divergence surviving that grounding is a terminal
state the record keeps. Persuasion is typed by kind: presenting a tradition the
author did not know of, or a demonstrated internal contradiction between their
implied disposition and the recorded graph, is admissible and expected;
persuasion by emotional manipulation is not.

**AI support.** It is the author's own words, and it dissolves the probe that
produced this node rather than answering it: the right to end a round cannot pass
from the checking party to the checked one by persuasion if agreement was never
what ended it. It is a well-populated family in tradition, and the closest member
is CPR 35.12, whose (3) directs experts to set out the issues they agree on and
those they disagree on "with a summary of their reasons for disagreeing" and
whose (5) makes any agreement they reach non-binding unless the parties expressly
agree to be bound — divergence recorded with its reasons, and agreement expressly
not the point. RFC 7282 §3 has rough consensus "when all issues are addressed,
but not necessarily accommodated", and §6 makes "the existence of the unaddressed
