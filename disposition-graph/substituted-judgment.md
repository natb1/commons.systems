What makes the borrowing more than an analogy is that the condition the standard treats as the hard case is the one this record is built to satisfy. Substituted judgment fails, and courts fall to best interests, when the person's values cannot be recovered, which is Saikewicz's difficulty and the reason the standard is criticised as a fiction in the cases where it is most needed. Here the values are not merely recoverable, they are the artifact: a graph of the author's standing dispositions is the advance directive that the medical setting almost never has. The record is therefore in the tradition's easy case and should take its preferred standard rather than its fallback, and an answer written under a deferral can say which recorded disposition it was read from, which is the check the standard implies and the medical setting cannot run.

**AI divergence.** The tradition assumes the person's values were recorded by the person, or by someone with no stake in what they say. Here most of the record's prose was drafted by the AI from the author's words and confirmed by nobody, so the surrogate reads back a record the surrogate largely wrote. Substituted judgment has no account of that case, and the analogy is weakest exactly where the record is thinnest, on the nodes whose text the author has not read closely. The change is still an improvement on the clause it replaces, because reading from the record is at least checkable and unbounded judgment is not; but it does not make the reading disinterested, and the record should not claim it does.

The tradition also carries a caution the record does not adopt and should name. Saikewicz applies substituted judgment to a patient who never had the capacity to form the wish, which most commentators regard as the standard stretched past its warrant, since attributing a choice to someone who could never have made one is attributing rather than recovering. The parallel failure here would be a deferral on a question the author has no disposition bearing on at all, where reading the answer off the record would be the same fiction. `the-choice-is-the-record-read-back` handles that by leaving the AI's own judgment to reach what the record leaves open, which keeps the two cases apart in words; whether a sitting can tell them apart in practice is not something this reading settles.

## Answer

Where a decision passes from one party to another, the tradition does not let the
second party decide on its own view of the merits until it has tried and failed to
decide as the first party would have. The ordering is an advance directive where
there is one, then substituted judgment from the person's known values, then best
interests, and the last is reached only where the values cannot be recovered.

The record takes the ordering and not the setting. Under a deferral the AI's choice
is the option the author's recorded dispositions best support, and the AI's own
judgment reaches only what the record leaves open. An answer written under a
deferral therefore says which recorded disposition it was read from, which is what
makes the substitution checkable rather than merely asserted.

Where the record is silent the AI is in the tradition's hard case, and the record
does not pretend otherwise: attributing a choice to someone who has expressed
nothing bearing on the question is attribution and not recovery, and the standard
is criticised for exactly that stretch. The divergence recorded here is that the
values this surrogate reads back were largely drafted by the surrogate, which the
tradition has no account of.
