## Answer

Never as a disposition. A disposition is standing: it holds until it is re-answered through the interview, and nothing in it says "for now". What is meant to pass takes one of five recorded shapes or is not recorded at all, and each shape makes its own staleness visible.

A criterion, when the temporary thing is really a standing obligation. "Review every landing before exit" or "drain the legacy record" is not guidance that expires; it is a criterion on the node it serves, and the frontier derives that it is unmet. When it is met it leaves the frontier and no text is edited. Transience is never stored; it is projected. A hazard learned in use is evidence on the criterion it qualifies, not guidance.

A shim, when an artifact or a text stands in for a materialization not yet made. A shim is declared where it comes into being, never retrofitted: on the node it instruments or projects, as that node's interim instrument or projection, naming the artifact and the condition under which it is removed, recorded verbatim and never paraphrased. A shim with no liquidation condition is not a shim but an undeclared permanent; a shim whose condition is met and which still exists is a frontier item. Shims are applied by default and bypassed only when asked. The notice an artifact carries is projected from the declaration.

An alternative, when the thing is a candidate answer pending the author's ruling: a drafted answer, a second answer the author's words or the clean-context review put on the table, a review finding excluded from a landing. An alternative lives in the dialogue state of the node it would answer, with its source, as the dialogue node says; it has no authority and dies at the ruling, into the answer, into the rationale as a rejected alternative, or into nothing. A proposal is the alternative that arose outside alignment, from an instrument, a signal, a criterion, or a conflict found in reconciliation, as the authority node defines it, and it opens the dialogue on its node. The alternatives pending are the content of the author's review queue, which the unanswered nodes are.

An un-aligned disposition, when the author has stated a disposition, or the AI holds evidence that needs the author's clarification, and the alignment dialogue on it has not concluded. It is a node with a question and no answer, hidden from the browser and listed by the alignment page. It carries the dialogue as the dialogue node defines it: the author's words, verbatim and dated, in a `## Disposition` section; the AI's account, its evidence, findings, and the reasoning behind the recommendation's facts, in an `## Account` section; the alternatives pending, each with its source, as data with a subsection each; `stage`, the next movement of the dialogue owed on it, periagogic, maieutic, review, or ruling; and, from the review stage on, the recommendation with its facts and its pin, and the review's state, as data. The position is stored because it cannot be re-derived: what the author has said in the dialogue exists nowhere else, and it must survive the session that heard it. Every function of the un-aligned disposition reduces to this. An owed reading or a surfaced term is not a node until it is written; it is a frontier item derived from the rationale that names it. An un-aligned disposition is a node like any other, carrying a question and the dialogue state on it, and it may be refined by children as any node may; what it lacks is not standing but authority, and it carries none for reconciliation unless the author grants bootstrap authority explicitly. At its recording it becomes an answer, or an amendment quoted into the node it refines; pruning remains an outcome the author may rule for, as it is for any node, and is not a property of the shape. A node with an answer carries the same fields while its dialogue is open, which is every unanswered node, as the unanswered node defines the status, and a ratified node whose ratification is under review; the browser shows such a node as it stands, with its stage.

Evidence, when the thing is an observation: a dated assessment reading, a measurement, a survey, a quoted artifact. Evidence is minted and never edited, superseded by later evidence, and cited by the node it bears on with a pin of what was read; it is neither disposition nor guidance, and a measurement no node cites is unsupported and pruned by default.

Not recorded: operational and session state. What is claimed, what is in flight, at which step a bite stands, and what a session holds back until the author has committed are derived when needed from the record and the implementation, or kept in the operation's own scaffolding and disposed of with it. The record holds no expectation about work in progress. The one position it does hold is that of an open dialogue, above, because only the record can carry the author's words across sessions.

One shape is forbidden by name: a node that records a unit of work, a plan, a task, or a step list, whatever it is called. A shim's liquidation condition and the survey in an account are not that shape: neither can be claimed and neither has a phase; the one is a condition the frontier derives from, the other evidence. Work is the derived difference between disposition and implementation; a bite is planned when it is claimed and exists only for the claim. A node whose achievement would delete it is not a disposition and is not written.
