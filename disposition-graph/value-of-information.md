## Answer

Supports both of the options it bears on, and supplies the quantity the record does not have. A test is worth its cost in proportion to the probability that it changes the decision multiplied by the value of that change; accuracy that never flips an outcome is worth nothing, and the calculation is done before the test is bought. The price of a test is knowable in advance and its value is not, which is why the theory's normal use is to ask what a measurement would have to show before it paid for itself.

The record adopts it first as a decision rule under ignorance. The differential the calculation needs here is what a weaker reader would miss that a stronger one catches, and the record has never measured it; what it has is the price, bounded and affordable, and the consequence of a miss, which is an author's ruling and an irreversible step. Under an unmeasured yield, a bounded price and a costly miss, the strongest reader on every reading is the allocation to make, which is `fable-for-both-readings` argued from the theory rather than from the author's words.

It is adopted a second time for the return. `fable-until-the-yield-is-measured` is the same rule with its successor written in: the strongest reader on both readings until the cost and the yield of each are measured per reading, the yield being the findings that changed a verdict, and a rule sized to that measurement thereafter. That is the theory applied twice rather than once, and the measurement it names is the instrument the answer declares. The tradition does not choose between the two options, because both are it; what separates them is whether the return is written into the answer or taken by interview, which is a question about how this record changes an answer and not about what a test is worth.

The tradition's own conditions are not met, and the reading says which. The calculation wants a prior over the errors a reading would catch and a value for each, and the record has neither, so what is used here is the decision rule the theory prescribes when the differential is unknown and not the calculation itself. And the value of a reading here is not only detection: the reading is also the warrant the author's ruling rests on, and a warrant's worth is not a probability of flipping an outcome. The theory prices the first and is silent on the second.
