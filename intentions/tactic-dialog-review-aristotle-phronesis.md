### 2. The end-setter gate (origination; gates Q2 and Q4)

At the sitting's finale the author's probes 1/2/5 construed: sophia
holds/knows the end; phronesis is deinotēs applied toward it. The correction
was issued but never formally confirmed (the author's only response was the
global "all of these outcomes sound correct but…"). The text says otherwise
four times: 1144a7–9 (VIRTUE makes the skopos correct; phronesis the things
toward it); 1145a4–6 (the practical pair is virtue + phronesis; sophia is
not in the practical loop); 1144a28–30 (the eye of the soul comes not
without virtue — formed character holds the end); 1144a3–6 (sophia relates
to eudaimonia as constituent — "as health produces health" — not as
knowledge-of-a-target). Consequence: "vice destroys phronesis because it
destroys sophia" inverts the text — vice cannot touch sophia (1140b13–15);
it distorts the PRACTICAL archai (1144a34–36); the author's own VI.5.3
articulation had it right. The sophia-sets-the-end construction is the
Socratic half-error VI.13 adjudicates (1144b19–21: the virtues are not
phronēseis/logoi — but not without phronesis; meta logou, not merely kata
logon).

**Exercise: the author re-articulates, unprompted, who sets the end and what
vice can and cannot touch.** Pass → Q2/Q4 may ratify. Fail → re-run the
comprehension dialectic on VI.12–13 before any ratification.

### 6. Author articulations (wording wins; preserved verbatim)

1. **III.4 spoudaios-as-measure:** "The spoudaios himself is the standard of
   measure of the good. Whatever the spoudaios wishes is the measure of good
   (which is not the same as the good is whatever the spoudaios wishes.)
   i.e. the good is external to the excellent person, the excellent person
   is the standard of measure for knowing the good."
2. **VI.5.3 (what vice does):** "temperance - the sustained knowledge and
   desire to act on the right mean of pleasure and pain - preserves prudence
   because prudence is knowledge of how to act towards (arche) oneself. Vice
   in temperance makes the principle of self the pursuit of pleasure or the
   avoidance of pain. Only in the mean can knowledge of the self as arche be
   seen clearly without being conflated with the principle of pleasure or
   pain. vice in temperance constrains prudence to what is achievable in
   pleasure or pain, not what is achievable in self. The language of
   destruction/corruption refer to this constraining of what is apparently
   achievable." (Sharpened per correction 7; vindicated by 1144a34–36.)
3. **The two-remove model (Q3's resolution):** "Both hexis could
   theoretically be represented on the graph (and sometimes encoded into the
   tools that operate on that graph), even if an imperfect model of author's
   hexis, which is an imperfect model of the good."
4. **Transitive desire (R14):** "while the claude agent has no desires,
   mounting claude as a delagatee does involve mounting transitive desires…
   if I were to delegate to a human I would be mounting their desires
   directly. The work of philosophical mobility is to manage that desire to
   prevent capture."
5. **Periagoge-shaped artifact (function c):** "the artifact is not just a
   database of facts, it is periagoge-shaped, the transmitted artifact
   functions though dialectic engagement. In the same way I build the graph
   by first delegating to, then engaging with the traditions, the artifact
   is first deferred to thereby prompting dialectic engagement."

(R1's framing and R3's no-timeout rule, at the cave dialog, are also author
wording.)
