# Reading chunk 14 (candidate): Pettit, Freedom as Antipower — non-domination as the structural reading of capture the heteronomy doctrine lacks

One office-hours sitting, at most 30 author-minutes. Candidate chunk of
`strategy-complete-grounding`'s first batch: no tradition record exists yet —
this sitting decides whether one should.

## Text

Pettit, "Freedom as Antipower" (*Ethics* 106, 1996). If the essay is
unlocatable, *Republicanism*, ch. 2 covers the same ground at greater length.

## Questions to establish relevance

- The graph reads capture through Kant's heteronomy — the will actually
  legislated from outside. Pettit's claim is stronger: the *standing power*
  of a counterparty to interfere arbitrarily is the unfreedom, even while
  benevolent and unexercised. Does non-domination ground why
  `virtue-progressive-detachment` binds under well-aligned delegatees better
  than heteronomy does ("even aligned attachments accumulate exit cost")?
- If adopted, does the irreversibility axis warrant a `chosen_over` against
  `tradition-kant` — Pettit for the structural axis, Kant for the divergence
  axis — or dual attribution without conflict?
- Antipower is *resilient* non-interference: protection that does not depend
  on the stronger party's goodwill. Is that the design test the recovery-path
  doctrine (`strategy-exercise-recovery-paths`) already applies?
