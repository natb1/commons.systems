# Reading chunk 15 (candidate): Ostrom, Governing the Commons ch. 3, with Mauss, The Gift as contrast — commons governance and gift terms for a project named commons.systems

One office-hours sitting, at most 30 author-minutes. Candidate chunk of
`strategy-complete-grounding`'s first batch: no tradition record exists yet —
this sitting decides whether one should.

## Text

Ostrom, *Governing the Commons*, ch. 3 (the design principles). Contrast
text: Mauss, *The Gift*, introduction and ch. 1.

## Questions to establish relevance

- The project is named commons.systems. Do Ostrom's design principles
  (participant monitoring, graduated sanctions, nested governance, low-cost
  conflict resolution) ground `strategy-distribute-workflow` and
  `strategy-commons-income` — the forkable-practice strategies — and
  `strategy-reversible-institution`'s defense against the ratchet?
- Mauss holds that gifts bind and obligate the recipient. The graph's "gift
  that transfers capability, not dependency"
  (`strategy-open-source-as-gift`, `strategy-commons-income`'s "gift terms
  only") is a deliberate divergence — does it survive his account, or does
  every gift carry the obligation he describes?
- Is one record right here (a commons/gift tradition) or two (Ostrom
  adopted, Mauss diverged-from)? The interview decides.
