# Reading chunk 16 (candidate): Buddhist non-attachment — the raft simile and the Second Noble Truth: the deeper form of the challenge Stoicism already lost

One office-hours sitting, at most 30 author-minutes. Candidate chunk of
`strategy-complete-grounding`'s first batch: no tradition record exists yet —
this sitting decides whether one should.

## Text

Alagaddupama Sutta (Majjhima Nikaya 22) — the snake and raft similes.
Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Samyutta Nikaya 56.11) — the Second Noble Truth
(tanha, craving; upadana, clinging).

## Questions to establish relevance

- `virtue-philosophical-mobility`'s own name refuses detachment-as-telos, and
  `tradition-stoicism` is the declined tripwire. Non-attachment is more
  central to Buddhism than apatheia is to Stoicism — does the craving/clinging
  doctrine add a challenge apatheia did not already pose, or is it the same
  refusal at greater depth (expected origin: declined, kept auditable)?
- The raft simile — the teaching itself is a raft to abandon after crossing —
  is anti-capture doctrine applied to one's own tradition. Keep the fragment
  while declining the doctrine, the premeditatio-malorum shape recorded on
  `tradition-stoicism`?
- The graph manages attachments because the household shares them
  (`virtue-alignment-of-attachments`). Does the Buddhist account of clinging
  as the root of suffering say anything about *shared* attachments the
  Stoic record missed?
