# Reading chunk 33: Aristotle, NE X.4 and Metaphysics Θ.6 (energeia vs kinesis) — verify the layer-placement grounding on kind-tactic

## Text

Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* X.4 (1174a13–1174b14) — pleasure is complete
at every moment, unlike a motion (a kinesis) such as building, which is
incomplete while under way and complete only when its end is reached — and
*Metaphysics* Θ.6 (1048b18–35) — the tense test: at once one is seeing and
has seen, is understanding and has understood; but not at once building and
having built. Two short passages; at most 30 author-minutes.

## Questions to re-open against the text

- `intentions/kind-tactic.md`, 2026-07-21 layer-placement clarification: the
  Claude-drafted gloss maps energeia onto persistent-layer content (a standing
  requirement, complete at every moment of its holding, never finished) and
  kinesis onto tactic content (completable, aimed at a terminal state whose
  reach deletes the node). Does the mapping hold against the texts, or does it
  strain them — e.g. is Aristotle's contrast about *activities and processes*
  in a sense that resists transfer to *requirements and tasks*?
- `intentions/tradition-aristotle.md` `adopted` — hexis-in-energeia (ratified
  2026-07-13 chunk 2) grounds the persistent half already. Does adding the
  kinesis contrast warrant a new adopted entry on that record, or is the
  existing hexis entry sufficient and the kinesis citation decoration?
- The tense test (Θ.6): does "achieving everything currently under it leaves
  a standing, condition-monitored posture" (kind-tactic's authoring test)
  correspond to the energeia side of the tense test as Aristotle draws it?

Two standing rules from `intentions/strategy-philosophical-grounding.md`
clarifications apply: the borrowed-articulation capture question and "the
reading wins" on any contradiction.
