clarifications:
  - question: What did the chunk-2 reading (NE II.1-7) ratify about habituation?
    answer: "Ratified at chunk 2 (II.1, II.4). 1103a14-26 assigns virtue of
      character to habit, not teaching. The author's articulation: a teacher is
      required for good habituation — 'otherwise no teacher would be needed, but
      everyone would be born a good or a bad craftsman' (1103b12) — but
      transmits no knowledge that constitutes the virtue; as with Republic
      518b-d's periagoge (chunk 1), the teacher turns the practice, directing
      which acts get repeated; the doing forms the state. Virtue of character is
      in this sense a social act: the same activity ruins or realizes the state
      depending on guidance (the harp, 1103b8-12), and the right education is to
      delight and be pained by the right things from youth (1104b11-13, citing
      Plato). II.4 completes the ratification: the circle — doing just acts
      before being just — is entered by chance or at another's direction
      (1105a22-23; the author's guidance reading, vindicated by the text), while
      the doer is just only when acting knowingly, choosing the acts for their
      own sakes, from a firm and unchanging state; knowledge counts for little
      or nothing where the other two count for everything (1105b1-3), and those
      who take refuge in arguments are patients who hear the doctor and follow
      none of the prescription (1105b12-18). The sign of the formed state is the
      pleasure supervening on the act (1104b3-8) — distinguishing virtuous
      action from virtuous character. Recorded 2026-07-13 /reading-review chunk
      2."
