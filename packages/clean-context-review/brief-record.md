## The record

The disposition graph is at `disposition/` (manifest `disposition/disposition.yaml`; a node `commons.systems/<graph>/<slug>` is the file `disposition/<graph>/<slug>.md`), and beside the graphs the same ref carries one other kind of file: the ledger of the author's words, `disposition/words/<date>.md`, whose entries are its `## <n>` headings and whose addresses are written `words/<date>/<n>`. A node is one question and the options recorded on its facts, with frontmatter (`question`, `form`, `under` parents, `defines`, `shims`, `instrument`, `order`, `boost`, `cites`, `tier`, `source` and `bears` on a reading, and, while a dialogue is open on it, `stage`, `review`, `depends`, whose entries are either a node id or a node id and an option on that node's answer fact written `<id>#<option>`, and `probes`) and two sections, in this order:

- `## Facts` — one `### <fact>` subsection per fact, in the frontmatter's order, opening with the reason for what that fact recommends, and then, for the facts whose options are this node's own (`answer` and `persistence`), one `#### <option>` subsection per option. The facts whose choices are the record's own vocabulary carry none: `ratified`, `delegated` and `deferred` on `authority`, `keep` and `prune` on `existence`, mean the same on every node, so each option's sentence is the gloss beside the term in the `defines` of the node that defines it — `authority` for the three classes, `dialogue` for the other two — and no projection supplies a sentence of its own.
- `## Account` — the AI's account in prose: evidence, findings, the reasoning behind the recommendation, the subsections of earlier readings (`### Clean-context review, <date>`, `### Frontier survey, <date>`, `### Frontier finding, <date>`), replies, and what is open for the author. Which accounts this brief carries, and which it leaves out, its scope section above says.

**The answer is not a section: it is an option's content.** Each `#### <option>` subsection holds four things in order: the option's **sentence**, the prose before the first marker, saying what it would answer and why it is on the table; an `**AI support.**` paragraph and an `**AI divergence.**` paragraph, the cases for and against it the record has accumulated; and, under `**Content.**`, the node as it would stand under this option. Content is held whole, as one fenced ```markdown block carrying a node that answers the same question and carries no `## Facts` of its own, or named as a change: a `From: <option>` line naming another option of the same fact and one fenced ```diff block of hunks, applied strictly to that option's resolved content, the chain walked back to a content held whole and never cyclic. What a node answers is the resolved content of the option a ruling confirms, or, no ruling confirming one anywhere in the record today, of the option its answer fact recommends.

This is the **content encoding**, and every node is written in it: a node file carries no Answer, Rationale, Recommendation or Disposition section, and no fact names an option as the one whose text stood. The author's words are not copied onto a node either; an option references by address, in `supports` and `diverges`, the ledger entries that support it and that it departs from. Where this brief renders a node whole it still prints three headings the encoding emptied — Disposition, Rationale, and a Recommendation heading for a recommended node held apart from what stands — each saying only that the node has no such section; nothing in the record fills them.

**There is no stamp.** Every decision on a node is a **fact** with a list of the **options** the AI considered, each shown as viable or as passed over, and the node's class is read off the **rulings** recorded on those options, never stored (`viable-options`). Each `facts` entry is `{name, options, recommends, boldness, against?}`:

- `name` is one of exactly `answer` (its options are the candidate answers to this node's question, and it comes first), `authority` (the class a ruling confers: `ratified`, `delegated`, or `deferred`), `existence` (keep or prune, where a proposal to prune the node lives), and `persistence` (present only where the recommendation would change the node's shape). A decision the author would rule on separately that is none of those is a question, and a question is a node under this one. **Every node that carries facts carries an `authority` fact**, since that fact is how a ruling confers a class other than ratified; a node carrying no fact at all is one at the periagogic or maieutic stage, where nothing is proposed yet.
- each option is `{name, source, ref, status?, reason?, ruling?, supports?, diverges?}`. On the answer fact `source` and `ref` are required: `author` (the author's own stated answer), `ai` (drafted in alignment), `review` (raised by a reading like this one), or the id of the node, or the name of the instrument, that raised it; `ref` is a date, a graph commit, or what raised it. `supports` and `diverges` are lists of ledger addresses. `status`, where it is present, is exactly `passed`, and then `reason` is required and says why the option was passed over: **a passed option is one the AI holds dominated**, never the recommendation and never the confirmed one, carrying no ruling, and it stays on the list, because a candidate the AI considered and can name never silently leaves it; the author may rule for it all the same, and the recording then clears the status. Deleting the node is never an answer option: it is the `existence` fact, because an answer option is a candidate answer to this node's question and deleting the node answers nothing.
- `recommends` names the one option the AI recommends, with its `boldness` (`low`, `moderate`, `high`); boldness runs from the AI's own knowledge against the record, so **high boldness is low confidence**. Every fact carries a `recommends` from the review stage on.
- `against`, beside `recommends` and optional, is the AI's own case against the option it recommends: the strongest argument against the recommendation, on record from the AI that made it, beside the recommendation itself rather than folded into some other option's passed-over reason.
- `ruling`, on at most one option per fact, is the author's own act: `{response: confirm|edit, date, of, reason?}`, where `of` pins the hash of the recommendation the ruling answered and `reason` is the author's own reason for the ruling, in their words, optional and never the AI's. Only the author rules; the AI writes no ruling and no class for itself. Which option a fact's rulings confirm is derived from them, the one carrying the most recent `confirm`, and is stored nowhere.

The class follows: **ratified** when the answer fact carries a ruling; **delegated** or **deferred** when the authority fact carries that ruling; conferred by the nearest ancestor whose authority fact carries one where this node has none; **unanswered** when no ruling reaches it, and then nothing on the node acts and reconciling under it takes an explicit grant from the author. No fact in the record carries a ruling today, so every node is unanswered. This brief prints each node's class and where it comes from. A ratified node whose recommendation has **moved** since its ruling (the pin no longer matches) is a **proposal** and is back on the alignment frontier for re-confirmation.

The hash a pin names is derived per fact, from the fact's name, which option it `recommends`, that option's sentence and resolved content, reaching through the `From:` chain, and the ledger addresses it references, `supports` and `diverges`, and each option's name paired with its `status` where one is set. The node's hash folds the question in with every fact's hash. Outside it: a rival's sentence, source, ref, reason, references and content; boldness; `against`; the AI's accumulated support and divergence; and the accounts.

A **reading** (`form: reading`) records a tradition and says with `bears` which options it bears on: `{node?, fact, option, relation: adopted|diverged}`; "chosen over" is derived, not stored. Wherever this brief prints a node whole, it prints on each option the readings that bear on it.

`review` is the state of the two readings the clean-context review divides into. `verdict`, `strength`, `date` and `of` are the review of one draft, written together or not at all, with two optional keys beside them: `against`, that reading's own strongest counter-argument (validation 6), distinct from a fact's `against` above; and `commit`, the graph commit it read, which lets a re-reading diff the text it pinned against the node as it now stands. `survey` is the survey of the whole frontier and carries six keys: `date`; `commit`; `of`, the recommendation hash it judged the node against; `text`, the sha256 digests of the five sections its validations read (`question`, `answer`, `options`, `rivals`, `words`); `findings`, what it left open here; and `pairs`, the candidate pairs it read that touch this node. A block carrying `of` is a **judged pin**, and only such a block carries `findings` or `pairs`; one carrying `text` and no `of` is a **read pin**, written on a node the survey merely read, freezing its text against a later delta without counting it judged. Each `of` goes stale by itself; a node is ready for the author's ruling at the ruling stage with a forward verdict and both pins naming the recommendation as it stands.

`probes` is the list of questions the AI needs the author to answer before it can recommend, and whose answers are not themselves dispositions (`author-questions`): a probe asks what the author intends, whether or not they have yet said anything, and its answer is consumed by the recommendation it moves — spent there, binding nothing and standing nowhere; where the answer would have to stand the question is a node and not a probe (`probe-or-node`). Each entry is `{id, asks, fact?, why, discharges, source, raised}`: `id`, a slug unique on the node, since a reading, a session and the author cite the same probe across a list that changes under them; `asks`, the question in one line, put open and never as a choice between drafted answers; `fact`, one of the reserved four where the probe bears on one decision rather than on the node's ground, and absent where it bears on the ground; `why`, why the record cannot answer it, naming the locus the AI read and what that locus leaves open; `discharges`, what an answer would settle and which recommendation it would move, which is also the ordering; `source`, in the vocabulary an option's `source` carries; and `raised`, the date, which is what makes a probe's age visible. A discharged probe stays on the list carrying `status: discharged` and the `reason` it was discharged, in the shape a passed option's status and reason already have. `probes` is a part of the dialogue, so a node carrying it carries a `stage`, and it is stripped from the frontmatter before the standing text is hashed, with `stage`, `review`, `depends` and `facts`. Where a probe is asked is the maieutic session and never the alignment page, which carries how many probes are open on a node and nothing of what they say.

**What you are shown of a node.** A node given whole is printed as its id, file and question, a line of stage, rank, status and class, its facts in summary, its review state, and what it depends on and stands under; then the text that binds on it, which is that resolved content; then every fact with every option — source and ref, whether it is recommended (with the boldness), passed over (with the reason) or ruled, the readings that bear on it, and the whole of its `#### <option>` subsection; then the last `### ` section of its account, the earlier ones counted and left on disk. A neighbour is carried by what it answers: its question, its own resolved content, and its answer options by name, one line each, with any option whose `source` is the node under review carried whole, since that is the draft's own text. A judged node in the survey's brief is carried once: the author's words its options reference, by address and quoted at most once; the one answer that binds; each option by name, status and sentence; and every other option's content as its difference from that answer, given whole where a difference would not be shorter.

Every node is unanswered until a ruling reaches it; `stage` names the next movement owed: `periagogic` (the author's account not yet elicited), `maieutic` (the answer not yet drafted), `review` (the clean-context review of the draft), `ruling` (the author's confirmation owed).
