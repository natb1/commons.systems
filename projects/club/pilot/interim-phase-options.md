# Phase 0.75 — Interim commercial options (recorded, NOT decided)
**Options for a paid, commercial-scale bridge between the Phase 0.5 pilot and the Phase 1 site — to be considered if/when the pilot picks up steam.**
*Recorded Aug 28, 2026 (operator request). Status: **no decision has been made**; nothing in the pilot spec, the schedule, or the gates changes by this doc's existence. Companion to `pilot/phase-0.5-spec.md`, `business-plan.md` §7, `plan/schedule.md` Revision 6, `strategy/financing-alternatives.md`.*

---

## 1. The gap this phase would fill

The current plan steps from a free one-room residential pilot ($0 at stake) directly to the full commercial commitment (lease + build-out + reserve, ~2028). Three known weaknesses live in that gap:

1. **Scale:** the pilot at full utilization (7 sessions/week, one room) with a 50% graduation conversion seeds ~3.5 weekly sessions — under half the ~8/week the money-losing base case already assumes, and a seventh of the 25-slot commercial week. A perfect pilot validates one room; the site needs three.
2. **Evidence quality:** the graduation ask is a deposit against a site ~a year away. Paid behavior at a real commercial venue is strictly stronger G1 evidence — actual willingness-to-pay, no stranger's-house confounder, no IOU.
3. **The gap year:** converted groups must survive ~12 months between graduation (~May 2027) and opening (~2028) on a deposit. An interim phase carries them as active paying customers instead; the plan currently carries no attrition haircut for that wait.

What any interim option must buy, in order: real-price paid behavior from retained groups; the list kept alive and growing across the gap; demand tested beyond one room; operator reps at commercial scale (venue logistics, payment, insurance, no-shows). What it must never cost: the working-capital reserve, the residence's legal cleanliness, or the pilot's own momentum.

## 2. When to open this decision (suggested triggers — confirm at adoption)

Not before the pilot has steam. Suggested read: take this doc up at the **month-4 checkpoint** (pilot spec §5) if the retention floor (≥3–5 recurring groups) is met and fill is at or above the ≥50%-of-offered-slots aspiration — or earlier only if demand visibly exceeds the house's capacity (waitlisted groups, formats the residence can't hold). A struggling pilot should not fork the operator's attention into venue shopping; a strong one makes the graduation ask far stronger if an interim venue is ready to receive converts.

## 3. Option A — Paid hosting residency at partner venues

**Form:** rent a room by-the-night at existing commercial venues (game shops per the #6c referral relationships, breweries, arts-district spaces, church/community halls) and host paying groups there at §1a prices ($15–35/hr band). The operator runs the booking, hosting, and tea service; the venue supplies walls, occupancy, and its own insurance posture.

- **De-risks:** the price test (real payments, real venue), multi-room-scale demand, the gap year, venue-ops reps. Converts the graduation ask from "deposit for 2028" to "your slot moves to [venue] next month at real prices."
- **Costs/risks:** per-night venue rent must sit below session price or the phase burns cash; a bad room (noise, vibe) contaminates the demand read — churn there tells you little; scheduling is hostage to the venue's calendar; commerce begins, so the LLC needs general-liability coverage for off-site hosted events (diligence item, likely low hundreds to ~$1K/yr or per-event policies).
- **Capital scale:** near-zero fixed; suggested precommit at adoption — the phase runs roughly cash-neutral, net spend capped in the low thousands over its window.
- **Fit note:** the strict-free rule (D16-r) is about the *residence*; paid hosting at a commercial venue does not reopen the home-occupation/insurance/D7 analysis. Residence events can continue free in parallel or wind down — decide at adoption.

## 4. Option B — Licensed evenings inside an existing daytime café

**Form:** a fixed-fee or revenue-share license for an existing café's dead hours (~6–10 PM) — the operator's bookings-only evening program running in someone else's building. This is a dress rehearsal of D9 itself: hosted evenings, keyholder routine, catering attach, lockable-café logistics.

- **De-risks:** the *whole evening engine*, not just room demand — the operating day seam, staffing rhythm, and the café-as-front-door hypothesis at commercial register. Strongest possible pre-lease evidence; plausibly grows into the site itself (lease handover or acquisition from a tired operator).
- **Costs/risks:** partner dependency is total (their brand, their landlord, their tolerance); brand confusion if the host café's identity clashes; occupancy-classification and insurance questions surface early (the host's A/B occupancy and coverage must actually permit recurring evening gatherings — the F12 class of risk, now on someone else's certificate); hardest of the options to source — requires a willing café in a candidate corridor.
- **Capital scale:** ~$1–3K/month license fee territory; suggested cap at adoption — commit month-to-month or by season, never a term that outlives the evidence it exists to gather.

## 5. Option C — Graduated real-estate commitment (transition structuring, not a separate phase)

**Form:** when the gates do pass, shrink the cliff itself: pop-up/vacancy programs (Operation Storefront-adjacent), a ≤1-year sublease with option, percentage rent in years 1–2, second-generation restaurant space (already the top lever in `strategy/financing-alternatives.md`), or opening with café + 2 rooms and the third shelled until utilization justifies finishing it.

- **De-risks:** converts "commit ~$150K on gate evidence" into tranches committed as evidence compounds; pairs naturally with A or B (interim-phase groups become the day-one book of business for a smaller opening footprint).
- **Costs/risks:** short terms weaken TI negotiating position and can waste fit-out spend; a phased build-out must not compromise the accessibility and occupancy posture (buy/lease compliance, don't build it — the v0.1 lesson).
- **Note:** this is a modification of Phase 1's entry structure rather than an interim phase; recorded here because the choice among A/B feeds directly into which of these structures fits.

## 6. Option D — Prepaid founding memberships as the bridge instrument

**Form:** sell standing-slot founding memberships redeemable immediately at the interim venue (A or B) and transferable to the site at opening. Already sketched as community capital (`strategy/financing-alternatives.md`; precedent memo §7.4) — repositioned here as the transition tool: capital that is simultaneously demand evidence and a membership that never goes dormant during the gap year.

- **De-risks:** gap-year attrition (a member with live redemption keeps showing up), plus modest early capital.
- **Costs/risks:** prepaid liability and refund terms need a legal read before any money is taken (add to the attorney scope, #19-adjacent); only works layered on A or B — there must be somewhere to redeem.

## 7. Cross-cutting rules (hold regardless of option)

1. **The residence stays strictly free.** Commerce happens only at commercial venues, through the LLC. The D16-r legal collapse is preserved untouched.
2. **Gate discipline:** interim-phase paid recurring bookings count toward the G1 rooms bar as equal-or-stronger evidence than deposits — but that amendment to the bar's instrument definition is made in writing at adoption, not silently. The café bar is untouched by all of this; counts remain its only instrument.
3. **Failure-cheap principle:** every option carries a precommitted spend cap and an exit that costs a month, not a lease. An interim phase that fails cheaply before a signature is the plan working.
4. **Pilot floors and the graduation bar are never renegotiated by this doc** — an interim phase changes where converts go, not what counts as passing.
5. **Operator hours are the scarcest input:** any option adopted must fit inside the honest capacity grid alongside the pilot's own cadence, or explicitly replace residence evenings rather than stack on them.
