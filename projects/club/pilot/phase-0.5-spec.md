## Amendment A — Aug 28, 2026: max utilization as the pilot's operating goal

Operator decision, superseding the conservative-cadence posture below where marked:

1. **The goal is near-full utilization of the club space** — a session most evenings (~7 slots/week, ~28–30 events/month at steady state; daytime private). Rationale: max utilization = strongest market validation (engagement half of §1a), most operator reps, maximum qualified conversion opportunities at graduation, the deepest list for the community-fundraising long shot, and the best community-service narrative for grants and subsidized financing. §3.5's 1–2 events/week cadence is **superseded**; ramp discipline and the burnout/neighbor stop rules (§8) are **not** — the ramp builds from 2/week, and the block's goodwill still outranks the pilot.
2. **Two spaces are finished for purpose, not one staged room.** The 3rd floor is the club space (main room + hall + the future bathroom, now hosting-critical, + both decks); the 1st floor is the receiving space (library/café register; guests directed up the stairs; 2nd floor private and unseen). Scope, budget ladder, and DIY critical path: `pilot/space-finishing-plan.md`. §3.3's ~$1–2K staging soft cap is **superseded** by that plan's budget: start ~$5–6K, operator ceiling $20K, full DIY, escalation only against named blockers. Buy-for-migration still governs furnishings; fixed finishes are home improvement that stays with the house (D7-consistent).
3. **Space design is itself a named pilot goal:** learning to make a residential space read as "club room above a library/café" is direct practice for the commercial build-out, and the staged space is the marketing asset (photography → website → credibility).
4. **A marketing plan now runs the demand side** — program design (hosted clubs + operator-run house programs), outreach at precommitted volume (touch 40–60 groups over the window), and a website the operator designs/develops/hosts: `pilot/marketing-plan.md`. Utilization aspirations (slots filled ÷ offered ≥50% by month 3, ≥80% by month 5) sit **on top of** the §5 floors; **the §5 floors and §6 graduation bars are unchanged and remain the validation verdict.** House-program attendance is logged but never counts toward hosted-club metrics or graduation conversion.
5. **The fundraising long shot is recorded as positioning only:** max utilization builds the list and story that a future community-capital campaign (for the commercial site, through the LLC, never at the residence) could run on. Like grants: considered, not banked. Posture details in the marketing plan §4.

Everything else below stands as written.

## 1. Decision and rationale (revised)

**D16-r: host free recurring-group events at the Albemarle residence — no money changing hands — for at least six months, in parallel with low-capital Phase 0 diligence, before any significant capital commitment (LOI/lease). End the pilot with a graduation ask that converts retained groups into gate-grade evidence.**

The operating logic: **"If I can't coordinate an event for free, I can't do it for cash."** Free hosting is a necessary-condition test, and going free *flips the evidence asymmetry in the pilot's favor*: the paid form's failure was ambiguous (price? the house?), but failure to fill free slots from a prospect list with documented venue pain is meaningful negative evidence on the engagement half of §1a.

Why the free form replaced the paid form (Aug 28):

- **Expected pilot income was negligible by design** (~$700–2,500 gross over a capped window, roughly breakeven after insurance) — the caps that kept the paid form legally incidental also capped revenue. The paid form's cash was not worth its risk surface.
- **The residence price test was weak anyway** (the venue confounder): payment at a stranger's house measured the house as much as the product. The price test moves to the graduation ask (§6), where groups price the *actual* product.
- **Going free collapses the risk section.** No commercial use → no home-occupation question, no business-activity insurance exclusion, no D7 operational-firewall breach, no platform terms. Hosting a book club at home is having people over.
- **Neighbor relations invert from liability to asset.** Regular free community events at the operator's Little Italy home are #25 community-relations work in the preferred market, done early, in person. The neighborhood is part of the vibe being tested.
- **The 6-month window buys what a 10–12 week window couldn't:** seasonal coverage (fall → winter → spring — winter demand evidence on the rooms side, complementing D12's winter café counts), enough reps to actually learn event coordination, format breadth, and a deep list.

**Goals, in order (Amendment A adds 5):** (1) learn to coordinate events — the operator's first hospitality reps; (2) learn *what events work* — formats, group sizes, cadences, what a room needs; (3) build a retained-group list that de-risks opening and reduces startup losses (each retained weekly club ≈ <!--m:sessionMarginalAllInK-->$4.5K<!--/m-->/yr of owner comp at the real site, catering attach included — plan §6, generated from `model/model.mjs`); (4) at graduation, produce gate-grade price evidence via the conversion ask; (5) learn residential-scale space design for the club/library/café register, and build the list + story that grants and the fundraising long shot draw on.

**What stays rejected:** any Peerspace/Giggster listing (reintroduces commerce and the wrong segment), and any mid-pilot fees, cost-sharing, or "suggested donations" — strictly free preserves both the legal cleanliness and the clarity of the graduation signal. Guests may bring food/drink (potluck norm); the operator may serve tea — hospitality, not sales.

## 2. Legal and insurance posture (collapsed by the free form)

The Aug 27 findings are preserved for the record but are now largely moot:

- The STR regime never applied (City Code §48-1 scopes it to "sleeping or lodging purposes"), and with no fee there is no commercial use, so the §15-507 home-occupation analysis (3 visits/day, 10/week caps) no longer governs — private social gatherings are not an occupation. The #19 attorney read on §15-507 is **retired**; no attorney precondition remains for the pilot.
- **Remaining action (one phone call, not a gate):** confirm homeowner's liability coverage for regular social guests at current limits; consider a personal umbrella policy (typically a few hundred dollars/year) given weekly strangers-as-guests. Prudence, not a precondition. *(Amendment A: mention the newly finished third-floor bathroom and the every-evening cadence in the same call.)*
- **Caps are kept but recategorized** — they are now about neighbor relations and operator sustainability, not legality: groups ≤8–10, quiet by 10 PM, no alcohol with strangers, no signage, considerate-parking guidance, and steady cadence rather than spikes. *(Amendment A raises the cadence ceiling to ~7 evenings/week at steady state; the per-event caps, quiet hours, and no-spikes rule stand.)*
- **D7 status: firewall effectively intact.** No commercial use of the house, nothing pledged, house off the venture balance sheet. The only residual is ordinary social-host liability, addressed above.
- **DIY finish-out flag (Amendment A):** verify Baltimore homeowner-permit rules for setting bathroom fixtures on existing rough-in before buying fixtures (`pilot/space-finishing-plan.md` §5).

## 3. Design

1. **Channel: direct recruitment from the T2 census** (34 groups; ≥13 named prospects with documented venue pain — Panera backrooms, RSVP rationing, noise-failed venues, waitlisted home groups, a forming D&D group with no venue), plus referrals and neighborhood word of mouth. The offer is an easy yes: a free, private, staged room for your group's regular session. *(Amendment A: full channel stack, outreach volume math, and pipeline stages in `pilot/marketing-plan.md` §2.)*
2. **Founding-groups framing from the first conversation** — "I'm opening a café with private club rooms; I'm hosting founding groups at my place free while we build toward it." This is honest, it builds the brand before the lease, and it makes the graduation ask (§6) the natural next chapter rather than a bait-and-switch.
3. **The space: two floors, prepared before launch** *(superseded in scope by Amendment A — spec: `pilot/space-finishing-plan.md`)*. The 3rd-floor club space and 1st-floor receiving space are finished for purpose (flooring, paint/trim, functional guest bathroom, staged furnishing): as much of the bookstore/café vibe as a residence can carry — book wall v1, big table, warm pooled lighting, tea service. Budget: start ~$5–6K, $20K ceiling, full DIY; furnishings buy-for-migration.
4. **Format breadth is a goal, not a byproduct:** across the window, host at least 3–4 distinct formats — pen-and-paper RPG, book club, writing night, board-game night, movie night — and record what each needs (table space, sound, lighting, duration, setup/reset time). This directly informs room design and programming decisions at the site. *(Amendment A adds operator-run house programs — open TTRPG table, silent reading/writing night, print/zine salon — as fill + reps; logged separately.)*
5. **Cadence** *(superseded by Amendment A)*: ramp from 2/week toward a session most evenings (~7/week, ~28–30/month) — never faster than the coordination routine, the operator's energy, and the block's comfort can absorb. The marketing plan's ramp discipline governs.
6. **Every event is logged** (see metrics, §5): attendance, format, duration, setup/reset effort, what worked, what the group asked for, neighbor observations; house programs flagged `house`. The log appends to `validation/data.md` monthly.

## 5. Precommitted metrics (written before first outreach — do not renegotiate)

*The floors below are unchanged by Amendment A and remain the validation verdict. Amendment A adds **aspiration targets above them** (utilization ≥50% of offered slots by month 3, ≥80% by month 5; steady state ~28–30 events/month) — aspirations drive outreach volume and are logged, but missing an aspiration while clearing a floor is a fill-rate finding, not a pilot failure. House-program events never count toward the floors.*

- **Fill:** sustained ≥4 hosted events/month by month 2 of the run, sourced from cold outreach and referral, not personal friends.
- **Retention:** ≥3–5 groups on a recurring cadence (monthly or better) by month 3, holding through month 6. A free group that stops coming is a real negative — log why.
- **Breadth:** ≥3–4 distinct formats hosted; a written read on what each needs from a room.
- **List:** by graduation, ≥8–10 engaged groups and a contact list the opening can be marketed to. *(Aspiration under Amendment A: 10–14 distinct hosted groups in the pipeline.)*
- **Ops:** complete event log; setup/reset time trending down; a repeatable coordination routine the operator could hand to a hired host.
- **Checkpoints:** month 2 and month 4 — if fill is failing despite genuine outreach volume, diagnose (offer? formats? the house itself?) and adjust before concluding anything.

## 6. The graduation ask — where the price test lives

At the end of the window, every retained group receives the real offer: **a reserved standing slot at the future site at §1a prices** ($15–35/hr band; flat vs per-head-with-floor variants per the precedent memo's price-discovery design), evidenced by exactly what the G1 rooms bar already requires — **a deposit or a signed recurring-booking agreement.** Free attendance never counts toward any gate; graduation conversions do.

**Precommitted read:** ≥50% of retained groups converting = strong price evidence; 25–50% = mixed, examine which formats/segments converted; <25% = a genuine price-problem signal to be taken seriously at the gate. This is a *cleaner* price test than residence pricing was — groups price the actual product (a private room in a café), not the operator's dining room.

## 7. Interpretation rules (revised asymmetry)

- **Failure to fill or retain free slots is meaningful negative evidence** — the necessary-condition logic. A residual stranger's-house discount survives (staging and the neighborhood mitigate, not erase), so diagnose at the checkpoints before concluding — but a pilot that can't give the product away weighs against §1a at the gates.
- **Fill and retention success is necessary but not sufficient** — free demand proves engagement, not price. Price evidence arrives only at graduation (§6).
- **The §9 trigger still fires only on gate instruments** (rooms bar at G1/G2, now fed by graduation conversions plus any independent deposit evidence). The pilot informs the gates; it does not replace them.
- **Amendment A corollary:** high utilization achieved partly through house programs is operator output, not market pull — read the hosted-club pipeline, not the raw calendar, when judging demand.

**What the pilot validates:** engagement and the group pipeline; event formats and room requirements; the operator's coordination ability; residential-scale space design for the register; the anchor list; at graduation, price. **What it cannot validate:** the café bar (foot-traffic counts unchanged and continuing), the true commercial vibe premium, the walk-in funnel, daytime demand, and price sensitivity before graduation.

## 8. Stop and adjust rules

- Neighbor complaint → pause, talk to the neighbor, adjust (cadence, parking, end time) before the next event. Repeated complaints → rethink cadence or wind down; the block's goodwill outranks the pilot — **at ~7 nights/week this rule is load-bearing; the neighbors' open house and parking cards are its front end.**
- Any incident involving a guest → pause and review the insurance posture before resuming.
- Operator burnout signals (hosting reads as dread by month 3–4) → reduce cadence and note it honestly: the real site budgets hired hosts (D6/D9), but an operator who hates hosting is a finding about the concept's founder-fit worth recording. *(Amendment A mitigation: low-effort formats — silent reading night — are scheduled deliberately so filled ≠ exhausting.)*
- Checkpoint diagnosis (month 2/4) may adjust formats, outreach, cadence — never the metrics floors or the graduation bar.
- Time-box: the graduation ask happens no earlier than month 6; if momentum is strong the run may extend, but the ask may not be deferred past ~month 8 — an endless free program is a community program, not a validation instrument (and if that is what it turns out to be, that is §9-relevant information: record it).

## 9. Accepted costs of the 6-month rule (recorded honestly)

- **Opening moves to ~2028** (gates re-anchored in `schedule.md`). The plan's own posture supports this — the base case is negative at conservative marks and the plan's job is to find out whether the venture is viable, not to open fast.
- **Candidate-building inventory will turn over** during the window. Accepted: the ~2,200–3,200 sf target widens inventory in every market; tours continue informationally; no building is chased before the pilot verdict.
- **Amendment A adds:** finish-out cash (start ~$5–6K, up to $20K) spent ahead of validation — accepted because the fixed finishes improve the residence regardless of the venture's outcome, and the furnishings migrate; and a heavier operator load (build hours now, hosting ~7 nights/week later) — accepted with the burnout stop-rule as the honest limit.
- **Offsetting gains:** winter evidence on both engines banked before any signature (D12 satisfied in-stride); SBLP round-cadence pressure relaxes (D8 — acceleration only, now with time to spare); the T1 analog sampling and T2 census refresh cycles get multiple seasons of data.
