# Space Finishing Plan — Phase 0.5 Pilot Spaces
**Two-space DIY finish-out at the Albemarle residence: 1st-floor receiving space + 3rd-floor club space**
*Aug 28, 2026. Companion to `pilot/phase-0.5-spec.md` (Amendment A), `pilot/marketing-plan.md`, `schedule.md` Revision 6. Source geometry: as-built drawings — transcription in `reference/as-built-drawings-transcription.md`; images in the project's uploaded files and the operator's records.*

**Operator decisions (Aug 28):** full DIY labor · start at the cheapest workable scope (~$5–6K) · escalation ceiling **$20K** — spend above Tier 1 only when it demonstrably buys utilization or removes a blocker. Every dollar competes with startup capital; but note honestly that most Tier-1 spend (flooring, bathroom, paint) is **home improvement that stays with the house** — it holds value on the residence side of the D7 firewall regardless of the venture's outcome. ## 2. Design intent — "club room above a library/café," not "room in someone's house"

The two floors play two registers, and the route between them is choreographed:

- **1st floor = the café/library register.** Warm, bookish, semi-public. A reception moment (small console table as a "front desk," guest book, coat hooks, umbrella stand), one visible wall of real, curated books, the kitchen glimpsed at a distance with the kettle going. The message on entry: *you have arrived at an establishment.*
- **The climb = transition.** Continuous flooring/runner treatment up the stairs, wayfinding by light — each landing slightly brighter toward the top, art hung stairwell-gallery style (the first **Odd Prints** surface). The 2nd-floor landing: door kept closed, landing kept bare and dim, nothing that invites a pause.
- **3rd floor = the club register.** Denser, warmer, more private than downstairs. Big table under a real pendant or picture-lit wall, the book wall v1, dark accent paint (library green / oxblood — paint is the cheapest register-changer available), tea service set up before guests arrive.

**Kill the residential tells** (costs almost nothing, matters most): no personal photos or domestic clutter anywhere on the guest route; no visible laundry/toiletries in the guest bath; branded-feel touches instead — a consistent typeface on small printed cards (matches the website), a branded guest wifi SSID, a printed "house guide" card on the table. Lighting rule everywhere: **2700K warm, multiple low pools, dimmers, never a single bare overhead.**

**Sound and neighbors:** rowhouse party wall — rugs on every floor, felt pads on every chair leg, quiet-by-10 norm printed in the house guide.

### Tier 2 — Vibe and capacity upgrades (~$2–4K, during ramp, only as utilization proves out)

Tub surround finished; book wall built out properly; deck furnishing (two bistro sets — seasonal capacity is free square footage); window treatments; stairwell art rail + picture lights (Odd Prints exhibition v0 — art on the walls is brand validation, not decor); upgraded tea station; game/event kit library (the TTRPG shelf earns its keep across multiple formats).

### Tier 3 — Escalation reserve (to the $20K ceiling, spend only against a named blocker)

Hired labor to un-stick a stalled critical-path item (a plumber-day is the most likely buy); flooring re-do if budget LVP fails; mini-split service; sound mitigation if the party wall becomes a neighbor issue; anything the month-2 checkpoint identifies as a fill-limiting space problem.

**Buy-for-migration rule (carried from the pilot spec):** furnishings, shelving, lighting, and tea service are chosen to be worth moving to the commercial site; fixed finishes (floors, bath, paint) are chosen for the house.

## 4. DIY sequence and critical path

Order of operations (dust flows downhill; paint before floors; trim after floors):

1. **Bathroom shell first** (messiest): board, tape, mud, prime. → 2. **Paint everything** (rooms, hall, stairwell, bath, accents). → 3. **Flooring** (3rd floor, then stairs, then 1st floor — the club room is the long pole for photography and first events). → 4. **Base/trim + repairs.** → 5. **Bathroom fixtures** (floor, toilet, vanity). → 6. **Kitchen cabinet repair** (parallel filler task any weekend). → 7. **Stage + furnish** (thrifting runs concurrently from week 1 — sourcing used furniture is a search process, start immediately). → 8. **Photo shoot** — gates the website launch and first invitations.

**Hours estimate (solo DIY):** bath shell 25–40 · paint 25–35 · flooring 20–30 · stairs 8–12 · trim 10–15 · fixtures 6–10 · cabinets 4–8 · staging 10–15 → **~110–165 hours.** At 12–15 hrs/week that is **8–11 weeks**: start ~Sep 1 → hosting-ready ~early-to-mid November. That is the ASAP-honest number; compressing it means more hours/week or a Tier-3 labor buy, not optimism.

**Critical path to event #1:** bathroom functional → club room floored/painted/staged → photos → website live → invitations out. The 1st-floor receiving space can trail by 1–2 weeks if needed (first events can be received adequately with paint + staging while its floor waits), but the bathroom cannot slip.

## 5. Flags and open items

- **Plumbing legality (verify, don't assume):** Baltimore generally requires licensed plumbers/permits for plumbing work; setting fixtures on existing rough-in in an owner-occupied home sits in a gray zone many owners DIY. Before buying fixtures, spend 30 minutes confirming Baltimore City homeowner-permit rules for fixture-setting on existing rough-in. If a licensed hand is required, that is the first Tier-3 buy (~$500–900 for set-and-connect), not a project stopper. → added to the open-questions ledger informally here.
- **Subfloor condition** unknown until walked — budget swing item; check early (week 1) so flooring quantity/prep is known before ordering.
- **Ships ladder to roof:** keep guests off it — latch/cordon + house-guide line. Liability hygiene consistent with the insurance phone call (#5b).
- **Deck railings at 3'6"** are fine for guests; verify gate/latch at the ships ladder.
- **Homeowner insurance call before event #1** (already in the pilot spec) — mention the finished third-floor bath and regular guests in the same call.
