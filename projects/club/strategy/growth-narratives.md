# Growth narratives — parked for future capital conversations
*Written Aug 28 2026, from the framing discussion of the same date. Status: **dormant reference.***

> **Standing note — read before using or expanding this doc.**
> This document exists to be expanded **only if and when it becomes relevant to an investment, financing, or grant discussion**. It records narratives, not commitments. Nothing here is a plan input: **no decision in `business-plan.md`, `plan/schedule.md`, or the pilot docs may cite this document as justification**, no gate bar moves because a narrative would benefit, and startup viability is judged entirely by the existing G1/G2 gates and stop rules as if this document did not exist. ## 1. The framing ladder (settled Aug 28)

Three framings of the same venture, each true at a different altitude:

1. **The operating identity (current plan):** *the East Asian party-room / karaoke-box model, localized to US club and tabletop culture, with a café as the front door.* Private recreational time sold by the session against a fixed asset; occupancy economics; proven at scale abroad (HK party rooms, SpaceCloud) and in adjacent US forms (escape rooms, Mox, Dungeons & Javas). This is the framing the plan actually runs on and the one to use with lenders, landlords, and partners today. See `validation/precedent-research.md`.
2. **The demand-side lens:** *Meetup's customer, housed.* The §1a unit — a small recurring interest group meeting 2–5 hrs in the evening — is exactly the unit the group-formation platforms (Meetup, Discord, FB Groups) enumerate and then leave homeless. The platforms are the venture's **upstream supplier, not competitor**; their public listings are a free demand census (`validation/runbook.md` T2).
3. **The growth narrative (dormant; §2 below):** the combination of 1 + 2 as a replication story — a proven venue unit plus a data-driven site-selection instrument built from the platforms' own demand map.

## 2. Narrative A — "the platforms built the directory; we build the rooms" (equity / growth capital)

**The claim, unpacked:** recurring-group recreation is a large, already-enumerated demand pool; the platforms that enumerate it never captured the value because they externalized the scarce input — the room; a replicable venue format that houses this demand captures what the platforms couldn't. Given a validated first site, this becomes the standard multi-unit hospitality pitch: **proven unit economics + a repeatable site-selection method**, where the T2 census technique is the unusual part — recreational-group density of any metro or neighborhood is measurable from public platform data *before a lease is signed*.

**The category proof to lead with:** escape rooms (zero → ~2,000+ US venues in a decade on the private-recreational-room transaction), Mox Boarding House (multi-city compound of the same shape), karaoke/party-room markets in Asia. **Escape rooms, not Meetup, are the scaling precedent named in the pitch.**

**How to say it — and how not to.** "Meetup as a real-estate business" is the private shorthand, not the pitch line. Said verbatim it invites the rebuttal that both halves of the sentence have famous corpses: Meetup never found a durable model (WeWork bought high, sold low) and Breather — rooms-by-the-hour as a venture-scale bet — liquidated. The defensible formulation keeps Meetup one layer down: *the demand map, not the business model.* "The platforms built the directory of our customers; we build the rooms they can't find."

**Audience match.** Fits multi-unit hospitality and franchise investors, family offices, community-development capital, and growth equity that underwrites unit P&Ls. **Does not fit venture capital** — a capacity-capped rooms business has no network effects, software margins, or winner-take-most dynamics, and the Meetup name is precisely what would attract the wrong check. The honest network-ish layer available at multi-site scale — cross-site membership, group directory, programming brand — is the SoulCycle/Barry's pattern (community brand attached to real estate), and should be pitched as that, never as platform economics.

**Prerequisites before this narrative may be used (all three):**

1. **A positive unit P&L, observed** — not a passed demand gate, not the gate-clearing row of the sensitivity table. The plan's own conservative base case is negative; the narrative unlocks only when the operating site actually clears (~$30K+ owner-comp equivalent, or a demonstrated trajectory past it).
2. **Founder-independence evidence.** n=1 founder-operated is the weakest validation point for a replication story; the diligence question is "does this work without Nathan hosting every night?" One other operator running even one night successfully is worth more here than another quarter of revenue. (Same founder-dependence question the pilot spec §8 already tracks.)
3. **The census instrument written up reproducibly** — T2's method documented well enough that a second market's density scan is a mechanical exercise (largely done; keep it current).

**Known tension, to be resolved *at activation, not now*:** replication capital will want the benefit-LLC's commons-as-budgeted-philanthropy line justified or cut. Accepting such capital is therefore also a governance decision (§9 of the plan, `strategy/entity-structure.md`). The current plan does not pre-concede anything to keep this narrative available.

## 3. Narrative B — civic-infrastructure story (grants / nonprofit-adjacent / community capital)

The mirror-image narrative for mission money: *evening civic rooms are missing public infrastructure* — the library's meeting rooms close exactly when recurring community groups meet (verified: Pratt policy + T4 hours), and this venture supplies the evening layer the public system doesn't, with a free commons attached as budgeted philanthropy. Natural audiences: façade/storefront and Main Street programs, A&E district programs, community foundations, and the Phase 2 nonprofit arm's eventual funders (lending collection first, per §9). The community-capital instruments in `strategy/financing-alternatives.md` (regulation crowdfunding, prepaid founding memberships) already sit inside this narrative — capital that doubles as demand validation. **Same guard applies:** grant narratives must describe what the plan already is; no program is added to the plan to fit a funder's rubric.

## 4. What activates this doc

A concrete conversation with a named counterparty (investor, lender at growth scale, grant program) where a growth or replication story is asked for — and, for Narrative A, the §2 prerequisites met. Until then: no expansion, no pitch-deck derivatives, no plan edits sourced from here. Revisit-by date: none; this doc waits.
